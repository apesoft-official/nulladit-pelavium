# Canadian lawful-access and Terms of Use policy

**Policy status:** Project policy and proposed Terms of Use language. This is not a legal opinion, a guarantee of immunity, or a representation that any particular law applies to every Apesoft entity or NULLADIT operator. Obtain Canadian counsel’s review before adopting it as binding Terms of Use.

## Policy

Apesoft intends to operate NULLADIT in accordance with applicable Canadian law and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. The Charter is law; the NULLADIT Constitution and Aurora Covenant are project governance commitments. Neither project document can displace a valid court order or create immunity from Canadian jurisdiction.

When Apesoft receives legal process concerning NULLADIT, the intended procedure is to:

1. identify the issuing authority, legal basis, jurisdiction, scope, deadlines, and affected data;
2. preserve information when legally required;
3. obtain legal review where practicable and provide only information the binding process requires;
4. seek clarification, narrowing, review, or a stay where counsel considers the process unlawful, overbroad, or constitutionally challengeable and the law permits; and
5. document the decision and its scope to the extent disclosure is lawful.

Apesoft will not voluntarily promise universal access, install a universal decryption key, or make a blanket commitment to build a systemic vulnerability. This policy does not authorize obstruction, destruction of evidence, or disregard of a binding order. Technical capability matters: the project will describe accurately what information it holds or can access, without claiming that recipients lack copies or that past ciphertext is immune from future cryptanalysis.

### Bill C-22 status note

As of November 28, 2026, Bill C-22, the *Lawful Access Act, 2026*, remains proposed legislation at the Senate report stage; it is not yet in force. The House-passed text contains a provider-focused limitation on interpreting the proposed Act as requiring a service provider to decrypt user-encrypted information, subject to an exception where the provider supplied the encryption and holds the necessary information. The text also preserves existing authorities and assistance obligations. This is not a general immunity for individual users, and the bill’s status and text must be checked before relying on it.

Current Criminal Code s. 487.02 separately allows a judge or justice, in connection with specified authorizations or warrants, to order a person to provide assistance reasonably considered necessary to give effect to them. Whether a particular order can compel a person to decrypt their own data is a fact-specific legal question; this policy makes no categorical claim.

## Proposed Terms of Use language

> **Legal process and privacy.** We seek to minimize the information Apesoft and NULLADIT node operators can access. Where legally required, we may preserve or disclose information within our possession or control in response to valid legal process. We will assess the process’s scope and jurisdiction and, where appropriate and legally available, may seek clarification, narrowing, review, or a stay. We will limit any response to what the binding process requires.
>
> No statement in these Terms, the NULLADIT Constitution, or the Aurora Covenant guarantees that a user, operator, message, or service is immune from Canadian law or court orders. A signature or use of the service does not waive a user’s Charter rights. Encryption and distributed operation may limit what a particular operator can provide, but do not guarantee that a recipient lacks a copy, that all providers lack relevant information, or that ciphertext will remain secure against future cryptanalysis.

## Drafting and implementation requirements

- Do not say the Constitution or Aurora Covenant “enforces” immunity against government.
- Do not condition privacy protections on whether NULLADIT is accused of a serious offence.
- Do not claim that Apesoft is categorically outside Canadian law or that every order is valid.
- Distinguish current law from pending bills, and verify official status before publication.
- Do not publish these proposed Terms as final until Canadian counsel reviews the actual Apesoft contracting entity, services, data flows, and legal-process procedure.
