# Canadian lawful-access and Terms of Use policy

**Policy status:** Project policy and proposed Terms of Use language. It records Apesoft's intent to protect expression and privacy and to comply with applicable Canadian law. It is not a legal opinion, a guarantee of immunity, or a finding that a particular statute applies to every Apesoft entity or NULLADIT operator. Obtain Canadian counsel’s review before adopting it as binding Terms of Use.

## Expression, privacy, and legal framework

NULLADIT’s constitutional commitments and technical design are intended to protect users’ ability to communicate and to safeguard private communications and personal information. They are substantive project commitments, not empty disclaimers. The project will design for data minimization, user-controlled encryption, and resistance to unnecessary access, and will not treat a government request alone as proof that disclosure is lawful.

The Canadian Charter protects freedom of thought, belief, opinion and expression (s. 2(b)), life, liberty and security of the person subject to fundamental justice (s. 7), and security against unreasonable search or seizure (s. 8). The Charter generally governs government action; it is not itself a private contract that gives Apesoft immunity. Applicable private-sector privacy statutes—including British Columbia’s PIPA and, depending on the entity, activity, and data flows, PIPEDA—also govern personal-information handling. The specific statutes and obligations must be assessed for the actual operating entity and service.

Neither project governance nor privacy law should be presented as preventing every legally authorized request. Conversely, the project will not assume that every request is lawful, proportionate, or within scope.

## Legal-process policy

When Apesoft receives legal process concerning NULLADIT, the intended procedure is to:

1. identify the issuing authority, legal basis, jurisdiction, scope, deadlines, and affected data;
2. preserve information when legally required;
3. obtain legal review where practicable and provide only information the binding process requires;
4. seek clarification, narrowing, review, or a stay where counsel considers the process unlawful, overbroad, or constitutionally challengeable and the law permits; and
5. document the decision and its scope to the extent disclosure is lawful.

Apesoft will not voluntarily promise universal access, install a universal decryption key, or make a blanket commitment to build a systemic vulnerability. This policy does not authorize obstruction, destruction of evidence, or disregard of a binding order. Technical capability matters: the project will accurately describe what information it holds or can access, without claiming that recipients lack copies or that past ciphertext is immune from future cryptanalysis.

## Bill C-22 status note

As of November 28, 2026, Bill C-22, the *Lawful Access Act, 2026*, remains proposed legislation at the Senate report stage; it is not yet in force. The House-passed text contains a provider-focused limitation on interpreting the proposed Act as requiring a service provider to decrypt user-encrypted information, subject to an exception where the provider supplied the encryption and holds the necessary information. The text also preserves existing authorities and assistance obligations. This is not a general immunity for individual users, and the bill’s status and text must be checked before relying on it.

Current Criminal Code s. 487.02 separately allows a judge or justice, in connection with specified authorizations or warrants, to order a person to provide assistance reasonably considered necessary to give effect to them. Whether a particular order can compel a person to decrypt their own data is a fact-specific legal question; this policy makes no categorical claim.

## Proposed Terms of Use language

> **Expression, privacy, and legal process.** We intend to protect users’ freedom to communicate and their privacy through data minimization, user-controlled encryption, and limits on what Apesoft and NULLADIT node operators can access. We handle personal information in accordance with privacy laws applicable to the responsible entity and activity.
>
> Where legally required, we may preserve or disclose information within our possession or control in response to valid legal process. We will assess the process’s scope and jurisdiction and, where appropriate and legally available, may seek clarification, narrowing, review, or a stay. We will limit any response to what the binding process requires.
>
> No statement in these Terms, the NULLADIT Constitution, or the Aurora Covenant guarantees that a user, operator, message, or service is immune from Canadian law or court orders. Use of the service does not waive a user’s Charter or statutory privacy rights. Encryption and distributed operation may limit what a particular operator can provide, but do not guarantee that a recipient lacks a copy, that all providers lack relevant information, or that ciphertext will remain secure against future cryptanalysis.

## Drafting and implementation requirements

- Treat expression and privacy as core project protections; do not reduce them to a no-guarantee disclaimer.
- Do not say the Constitution or Aurora Covenant “enforces” immunity against government.
- Do not condition privacy protections on whether NULLADIT is accused of a serious offence.
- Do not claim that Apesoft is categorically outside Canadian law or that every order is valid.
- Distinguish the Charter’s constraints on government action from privacy statutes that may regulate private organizations.
- Distinguish current law from pending bills, and verify official status before publication.
- Do not publish these proposed Terms as final until Canadian counsel reviews the actual Apesoft contracting entity, services, data flows, and legal-process procedure.
