# NULL22 Representation and Node Service Compensation

**Status:** Apesoft policy statement and proposed operator standard. This document is not legal advice, a legal opinion, a contract amendment, or a determination of regulatory status.

## Apesoft representations

Apesoft will describe NULL22 as a protocol token used by the NULLADIT system, including as the unit in which eligible Proof-of-Service rewards may be denominated. Apesoft will not market or describe NULL22 as Canadian legal tender, government-issued money, an investment, a security, a deposit, a guaranteed store of value, or a promise of profit or appreciation.

These limits govern Apesoft's representations; they do not decide how a regulator, court, tax authority, or other person will legally classify a token, transaction, offering, or platform. A label in a contract or repository cannot create an exemption. Securities and other regulatory treatment depends on applicable law and the facts, including the rights and arrangements involved. Nothing here claims that NULL22 or any related activity is exempt from Canadian or other law.

## Proof-of-Service and node participation

The intended protocol arrangement is that an operator who voluntarily joins a reward-eligible Proof-of-Service program may receive NULL22-denominated rewards for verifiable network services, including delivery, temporary encrypted storage, or availability, under the applicable approved rules. Operators choosing to participate must expressly accept the reward terms through the applicable operator agreement or enrollment process. Publishing this document alone does not amend existing agreements, prove that any operator has assented, or change a deployed contract.

The project may describe this intended service-for-token arrangement as barter-like or as non-cash consideration. That description is not a legal determination. Proof receipts can evidence protocol work; they do not determine the legal, tax, or accounting treatment of a reward.

Operators decide whether participation is worthwhile. Electricity, bandwidth, hardware, and other operating costs may inform an operator's decision to accept the program's terms. Those costs do not, by themselves, establish NULL22's fair market value, tax value, exchange value, or legal adequacy, and no reward schedule guarantees profitability or conversion to Canadian dollars.

## Canadian tax and valuation

Canadian tax consequences depend on the transaction and the parties. The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) explains that using a crypto-asset to pay for goods or services is treated as a barter transaction for income-tax purposes, and that a vendor generally includes the value of the goods or services provided or the crypto-asset received, whichever is more readily valued. CRA guidance for GST/HST barter transactions refers to the fair market value of the property or service at the time of supply.

Parties may agree in advance on the number of NULL22 units offered for defined services. Their agreement does not override any mandatory statutory valuation, income-reporting, GST/HST, securities, or other legal requirement. This policy does not instruct operators to ignore external economic evidence where applicable law requires a fair-market-value or other valuation. Each operator is responsible for its own records, reporting, and legal obligations and should obtain independent advice when needed.
